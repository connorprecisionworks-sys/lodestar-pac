Lodestar PAC ranks near-Earth asteroids by estimated resource value against accessibility, predicts the composition of objects that have never been spectrally measured, and computes real launch windows. It is built to scale to the modern flood of discoveries, where hand-curation cannot keep pace. Every figure is an estimate with an explicit uncertainty band and a provenance tag — measured, ML-predicted, or assumed. It never presents an inference as a measurement.

01 — SystemFive layers, each independently testable

Data ingestion normalizes orbital elements, sizes, albedos, and measured spectral types from the JPL Small-Body Database into a store of 41,884 objects. A machine-learning layer predicts composition for the uncharacterized majority. An astrodynamics layer propagates orbits and solves transfers for launch windows. A ranking layer combines value and accessibility. An interface renders it all, with real shape models and per-object mission feasibility.

02 — CharacterizationPredicting composition from reflectivity

Of 41,884 near-Earth asteroids, only 267 have a measured taxonomic class. The model predicts an object's compositional complex — C carbonaceous, S stony, M metallic — with calibrated confidence, from the features that exist for far more objects. Albedo (reflectivity) is the physically strongest signal: dark surfaces indicate carbon-rich bodies, bright surfaces stony ones.

A class-balanced random forest (200 shallow trees) is trained on eight features: albedo, absolute magnitude, the three orbital elements, and three dynamical features (Tisserand parameter, perihelion, aphelion). The dynamical features were kept only because leave-one-out testing showed they improved accuracy, 82% versus 80% without.

82% accuracy under leave-one-out cross-validation, against a 72% majority-class baseline. The model learns the real dark-versus-bright structure that separates carbon from stone.

Applied to the catalog, the model assigns a confident composition to 571 objects with no measured spectrum, expanding the characterized population from 267 to 838 — roughly tripling it. Each prediction carries its confidence and is tagged ml-predicted, never measured.

Honest limitations

  • Metallic asteroids cannot be identified from albedo. The X-complex is albedo-degenerate and only 4 labeled M-types exist; the model reports M as a weak prior, never a confident call. Reliable metal ID needs radar or spectra.
  • Coverage is capped at ~3% — only ~1,200 objects have a measured albedo, so the model is silent on the rest. The central bottleneck.
  • Space weathering and grain size bias reflectance inference; the training set is small and skewed toward bright, well-observed bodies.

The mitigation is the same throughout: uncertainty is a first-class output. The path past the coverage cap is multi-band colors (SDSS, and at scale the Rubin Observatory), which exist for hundreds of thousands of objects; the pipeline is built to ingest them.

03 — AstrodynamicsReal launch windows, validated

States are propagated under two-body Keplerian motion; transfers are solved with a universal-variable Lambert solver (Curtis, Algorithm 5.2); a porkchop sweep over departure date × flight time finds the minimum-Δv window, including the launch burn from a 300 km parking orbit and the arrival rendezvous burn.

The Lambert solver reproduces the textbook case to four decimals, and the porkchop recovers 433 Eros's optimal Δv at 6.10 km/s against the published 6.11. The earlier closed-form proxy gave 7.6 — a real accuracy gain.

This is screening-grade: two-body analytic ephemerides, not the n-body precision a flown mission requires. It decides which target and roughly when, not how to fly the spacecraft.

04 — ValueFrom composition to resources to dollars

Value is built up from composition rather than asserted. Each complex maps to recoverable fractions of water, iron-nickel, and platinum-group metals at a coarse in-space value per kilogram; mass comes from size and bulk density. The result is per-object resource tonnages and a headline value with an uncertainty band that widens by provenance — tightest for measured composition, wider for predicted, widest for assumed. Order-of-magnitude figures for triage, not appraisals. It is why the lowest-Δv high-value objects the engine ranks are the real destinations: Apophis, Itokawa, Bennu, Eros.

05 — FeasibilityThe fuel-and-effort story

Each Δv becomes a propellant mass fraction via the Tsiolkovsky equation. At Eros's 6.1 km/s, a chemical stage must be 86% propellant by mass; an ion stage, 19% — the quantitative reason deep-space missions go electric.

06 — HonestyThe fence

Every estimate carries provenance and uncertainty. Composition is tagged measured, ml-predicted, or assumed; Δv is benner or computed; value and resources ship as ranges. The litmus test for every feature is whether it helps decide which asteroid to study or visit next. Anything drifting toward flying the mission is out of scope.
